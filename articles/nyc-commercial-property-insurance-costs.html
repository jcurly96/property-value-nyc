Why NYC Commercial Property Insurance Premiums Keep Rising

If your renewal notice keeps arriving with a larger number than last year, you're seeing a trend playing out across nearly every NYC commercial and multifamily owner's book of business. High insurance premiums on NYC commercial property are being driven by forces well beyond any single building's claims history, and understanding what's behind the increases helps you decide how to respond — and whether your building's economics still work at today's cost of coverage.

The National Forces Driving Premiums Up

Property insurers have spent the past several years repricing risk across the country. Reinsurance costs — the insurance that insurance companies themselves buy — have risen sharply following a string of costly catastrophe years nationally, and those costs get passed down to every policyholder, even in markets with no direct hurricane or wildfire exposure. Combined with persistent construction cost inflation (meaning claims cost more to settle than they used to) and rising litigation and liability costs, carriers have tightened underwriting and raised rates broadly across commercial lines.

NYC-Specific Risk Factors

On top of the national trend, several factors specific to New York City push premiums higher still. The city's older building stock — much of it pre-war construction with aging electrical, plumbing, and roofing systems — reads as elevated risk to underwriters. Local liability exposure is also a real factor: NYC's legal environment for slip-and-fall and habitability claims tends to produce larger claim payouts than many other markets, which carriers price in at renewal.

Local Law 97 as a New Underwriting Factor

An emerging factor some carriers now weigh is a building's exposure to Local Law 97 fines. A building that's clearly headed toward a compliance shortfall as the 2030 emissions caps take effect represents a future financial liability for the ownership entity — and some underwriters are beginning to factor climate-compliance risk into how they price and structure commercial policies, alongside the building's physical condition.

Unlike property taxes, there's no formal grievance process for an insurance premium — your only real levers are shopping the policy, reducing risk, or accepting the new cost structure.

Insurance vs. Taxes: A Cost You Can't Easily Appeal

Property taxes, however frustrating, come with a defined appeal process — certiorari, abatements, exemptions. Insurance doesn't work that way. A premium increase is a private market pricing decision, not a government assessment, so there's no formal grievance mechanism. That makes rising insurance costs a purer compression of NOI than taxes in some respects: the expense simply goes up, and the only real responses are on the risk-management side, not the appeals side.

Practical Options for Owners

A few steps can meaningfully affect your renewal, even if they can't reverse the broader market trend:

  • Shop the policy every renewal cycle rather than auto-renewing — carrier appetite for NYC risk varies significantly year to year.
  • Invest in risk mitigation — updated electrical panels, sprinkler systems, roof replacement, and documented preventive maintenance can all support better terms.
  • Review your coverage structure with a broker who specializes in NYC multifamily and commercial risk, since generic policies often carry gaps or unnecessary coverage that inflate cost.

When Rising Premiums Mean It's Time to Reassess

For some owners, rising insurance is a manageable line-item increase. For others — particularly older, larger, or higher-risk buildings — it's one more fixed cost stacking on top of taxes and financing costs to the point where the building's returns no longer justify holding it. The only way to know which category you're in is to look at the full picture: current NOI, current expenses, and what the building would actually sell for today.
